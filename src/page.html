    Connecting Meta

    When the app runs on its server (npm start), choose Meta ads and paste an access token from your Meta app (Marketing API → Tools, tick ads_read only, Get Token). Paste it only here, never into a chat or email. Access is read-only: nothing in your ad account can be changed.

    1. Pick the ad account. The first sync pulls the last 90 days. Auto-sync daily keeps it fresh; switch it off to sync only when you press Sync now.
    2. Choose dates and level: campaigns, ad sets or ads. The list shows everything that spent money in that period, biggest first, and ticks the top three with the same objective.
    3. Fill what Meta can't know, mainly qualified leads from your CRM. Meta's numbers are locked; your entries are kept across syncs.
    4. On Compare, use Save comparison to come back to the same picks later, or Share link to send a read-only snapshot. Manage links under Shared links.

    Meta connections last 60 days; the app reminds you a week before. Without a Meta app configured, the server runs in demo mode with a simulated account. Setup steps are in SETUP-META.md.

    Getting started

    1. Bring in campaigns. On Campaigns, choose or drop a CSV/Excel export. One row per campaign is ideal; daily or ad-set rows with the same campaign name are added together.
    2. Fill the gaps. Rows with a yellow Fill … gaps button are missing something the analysis needs. Most often that's qualified leads (from your CRM), universe (audience size from Ads Manager) and budget.
    3. Tick 2–5 campaigns and open Compare.
    4. Read top to bottom. The leader and ranking come first, then the charts, the four comparison steps, the funnel, common ground and differences, fixes for each campaign, and a plan for your next one.
    5. Download the report as HTML (open it and print to PDF), the numbers as CSV, or both as an Excel workbook.
    6. Set your own targets (CPL, qualified %) under Targets & settings so verdicts are judged against what you need, not only against each other.

    Exporting from Meta Ads Manager

    1. Open Ads Manager → Campaigns, pick the date range, and select the campaigns.
    2. Under Columns, include: Campaign name, Objective, Reporting starts, Reporting ends, Amount spent, Impressions, Reach, Frequency, Link clicks, Landing page views, Results, Result indicator, Leads, and Budget.
    3. Click Reports → Export table data and choose .csv or .xlsx.
    4. Optional: add your own columns Qualified leads, Audience size and Targeting in the spreadsheet before uploading. They are picked up automatically.

    Google Ads exports (Campaign, Cost, Impr., Clicks, Conversions) also work. Columns the tool doesn't recognise are ignored.

    Filling gaps

    Any blank or zero value that matters for a campaign's objective is marked needed in the editor. Leave a field empty if you truly don't know it; the tool skips metrics it can't calculate instead of guessing.

    • Universe: the "Estimated audience size" Ads Manager shows while you set targeting. Use the middle of the range.
    • Qualified leads: leads your team marked as worth following up. Without this, cheap-but-useless leads can look like a win.
    • Funnel levels: set automatically from the objective. Tick extra levels if a campaign deliberately aimed at more than one.

    Reading a comparison

    Campaigns are compared in the order a performance marketer would check them:

    1. Cost and budget: cost per result (CPL for lead campaigns) and how much was spent. This counts most (3 points).
    2. Audience and lead quality: universe size, how much of it was reached, qualified lead % and cost per qualified lead (2 points).
    3. Duration: days run and leads per day (1 point).
    4. Supporting signals: CPM, CTR, CPC, frequency and landing-page rates. These explain why the numbers above look the way they do (½ point).

    For awareness campaigns the first step uses CPM instead of CPL; for traffic, CPC; for sales, cost per conversion. If you compare campaigns with different objectives, the tool warns you, because they were aiming at different things.

    A "could be chance" notice means the difference between two campaigns is small compared with their sample sizes. Let them run longer before deciding.

    Reading the charts

    The charts sit just below the ranking, in the same order as the comparison. Each one ends with a plain-English takeaway. Hover or tab onto any bar or dot for exact numbers, or open Show as table.

    • Budget and spend: each bar is what was spent, and the black line is the budget. A bar well short of its line means the platform couldn't spend the money, so that campaign's results may be understated.
    • Share of spend vs share of results: compare a campaign's slice of the Spend row with its slice of the Leads or Qualified leads row. If the result slice is bigger, that campaign got more than its share for the money.
    • Cheap leads vs cheap good leads (lead campaigns only): the hollow dot is cost per lead, the filled dot is cost per qualified lead. A long line means you paid for a lot of junk leads. Judge on the filled dot.
    • Cost vs quality map: left is cheaper, up is better quality, bigger bubbles spent more. The grey lines mark the middle of the group, so the top-left corner holds the campaigns worth copying. Without qualified leads it uses click-through rate instead and says so.
    • Day by day (Meta data only): 7-day rolling lines for cost per lead, CPC, CPM, CTR, spend or leads. Hover for every line's value that day. A cost line climbing for a week or more usually means the audience is tired of the ads.
    • When they ran: run length, daily spend and overlaps. Two campaigns on the same platform at the same time can bid against each other for the same people.

    Charts that need data you haven't entered show a short note saying what to add. The downloaded HTML report includes the same charts.

    The funnel levels

    L1Awareness: reach and impressions. Judged on CPM and reach.
    L2Awareness: frequency. How often each person saw the ad; 1.5–3 is healthy.
    L3Engagement: clicks and views. Judged on CTR, CPC and cost per view.
    L4Engagement: landing page, lead forms and qualified leads. Judged on CPL, qualified % and cost per qualified lead.
    L5Conversions: sales or sign-ups. Judged on cost per conversion and ROAS.

    A campaign is aimed at the levels its objective targets. Numbers at other levels are a side effect: useful context, but not what the campaign should be judged on.

    Targeting types

    Open
    Almost no restrictions beyond location and age (e.g. Advantage+ audiences). The platform finds people itself. Cheap reach and leads, but quality can drop. Default: universe of 1 crore (10 million) or more.
    Loose
    A few interest, behaviour or demographic layers. A balance between cost and relevance. Default: between 10 lakh and 1 crore.
    Focused
    Narrow, stacked conditions, lookalikes, customer lists or retargeting. Higher CPM, usually better lead quality. Default: 10 lakh (1 million) or fewer.

    Targeting notes win over universe size: "Advantage+" means open; "lookalike", "custom audience" or "retargeting" mean focused. You can override the type per campaign, and change the thresholds in settings.

    Words you'll see

    CPL: cost per lead
    Amount spent ÷ leads.
    Qualified leads %
    Qualified leads ÷ leads. Below your target (30% by default) means you are paying for leads your team won't use.
    Cost per qualified lead
    Amount spent ÷ qualified leads. Often the truest measure of a lead campaign.
    Universe
    The estimated number of people the targeting could reach.
    Universe reached
    Reach ÷ universe. Above 60% with high frequency means the audience is used up.
    CPM
    Cost per 1,000 impressions. High CPM usually means a narrow or competitive audience.
    CTR: click-through rate
    Link clicks ÷ impressions. Under 0.8% suggests the creative isn't working; above 2% is strong.
    Frequency
    Impressions ÷ reach. Above 3.5 risks ad fatigue.
    Learning phase
    The first days of a campaign while the platform figures out who to show it to. Results are unstable then, so judge after a week or more.
    ROAS
    Conversion value ÷ amount spent. Below 1× loses money on ads alone.

    What it can't tell you

    • It sees totals, not creatives, so it can say a creative is weak but not which image to change.
    • Benchmarks (CTR under 0.8%, frequency above 3.5) are general rules of thumb across industries. Your own targets in settings matter more.
    • Recommendations are a starting point to discuss with whoever runs the ads, not a guarantee.
    • Everything stays in this browser. Clearing site data removes saved campaigns, so keep your export files.
